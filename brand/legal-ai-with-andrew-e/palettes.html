02 / How the third colour works

Six things the references teach.

1 / Neutrals step backThe two neutrals carry almost no colour, so the accent owns every bit of it.

Seven of the eight pair a near-black with a white, ivory, silver or grey. The best ones tint the neutrals slightly towards the accent: Lumora's ink black #031211 leans teal. Ocean Instinct is the exception. Its saturated blue ground turns it into a two-colour poster.

2 / Warm accents winFive of the eight accents are red or orange, with hue angles between 0° and 21°.

Warm, fully saturated colours advance against cool or neutral grounds, which is why they stop a scrolling thumb. The other three are blue, teal and lime.

3 / Small and deliberateThe accent appears in a few fixed places: one headline word, the hero image, one button and tiny rules.

It never appears in body text. By my visual estimate it covers about 5 to 15 per cent of the layout. Lime in "Design Without Limits" is the most disciplined: a full stop, a dash and a sphere.

4 / The image carries the colourIn every mock-up, the photograph or render supplies most of the accent.

Think satin, sapphires, a crab, red light, a car stripe, a sphere. You will not have product photography, so your accent has to come from graphics (marks, highlights, brackets) and from the video set. The presenter also matches her earrings to several palettes. That same trick works for you: one accent-coloured object in frame.

5 / The accent is not a text colourIn all eight, the accent fails as normal-size text against at least one of its own grounds.

Signal Red fails on both of its grounds (2.89 and 2.73). Neon Lime on ivory measures 1.19. These designers use the accent as a fill or as headline type. Your brand must assign text roles explicitly, because your audience includes people with low vision.

6 / Trend versus identityNear-black with a lone red pop is a widely used look right now.

Two references rely on chrome AI heads, which your avoid list rules out. One borrows a racing livery. Colour alone will not make you distinctive. The third colour needs a job that belongs to your brand: correction, highlighting or warning.

05 / Recommendation

Choose A, Proof. Keep B, Highlight, as the challenger.

Proof is the only palette where the accent passes as text on your main ground and reverses cleanly onto a panel. Its meaning, correction, is the core of what you teach. It also follows the reference palettes that clearly perform in the feed: Signal Red had 12.3K likes and Laser Crimson 8,873. Likes reflect each creator's reach and audience, so read them as context, not proof that a colour works.

Highlight is more distinctive, and its highlighter role suits your screen-recorded lessons. Test the two side by side on the same three posts before you decide.

Avoid: cobalt with black and white (back to the generic law-tech code), powder blue with orange (racing livery) and any palette that depends on chrome AI imagery.

Rules for the third colour

  1. One accent only. Never pair two accents in one asset.
  2. One word per headline. The accent marks the word that matters: wrong, check, source.
  3. It always has a job. Strike-through, bracket, highlight or button. Never a background flourish.
  4. Never body text, unless that pairing passes 4.5:1 in the table above.
  5. Put it in the room. Use a neutral wall in the light colour and one accent object in frame, such as a red folder or a lime highlighter. Wear neutral clothes.
  6. Tint the neutrals. Shift the ink a few degrees towards the accent so the palette feels related, as Lumora does.
Sources and limits
  • Primary: eight screenshots of uxui_shrut Instagram reels supplied by you. The hex values are read from the on-screen labels. Video compression can shift displayed colour, so sample the original files before final use.
  • One hex value (Neon Lime) is partly hidden by the like counter. I read it as #CDF27B, which is uncertain.
  • Contrast: the WCAG 2.2 relative luminance formula, calculated for every pair quoted. Thresholds are 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and graphics.
  • Area splits, stopping power and ownability are visual estimates and professional judgement, not measurements.
  • Like counts are as shown in the screenshots. They depend on the creator's reach, so they are not evidence that a palette performs.
  • Templates use the [E] Evidence route from the identity review so the colour is compared on a constant layout. Portraits are placeholders.
