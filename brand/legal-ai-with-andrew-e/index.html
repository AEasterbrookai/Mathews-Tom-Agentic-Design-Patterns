Brand identity review / Three routes Approach informed by Wolff Olins practice

A brand people can check.

The current identity documents are careful and well researched, but the design is generic. Navy, a soft blue, a sans-serif wordmark with an underline: it could belong to any law firm or AI start-up. This review keeps the strategy, finds the idea buried inside it, and builds three different identity routes from that idea.

01 / Diagnosis

Good strategy. Generic design.

I reviewed four documents: the Five Palette edition (14 pages), the Colour Brand Identity Report (9 pages), the Research and Platform Foundation report and the Social Media Strategy 2026. Each point below is drawn directly from those files.

There is no brand ideaThe four qualities are credibility, independence, approachability and practicality. These are adjectives, not an idea. Swap in any legal tech consultancy's name and every page still works.Without an idea, design decisions come down to taste. That is why the documents end up comparing palettes rather than concepts.
It looks like everyone else in law and in AIFour of the five palettes are navy or graphite with a blue accent. Navy signals law firm. Blue signals tech. Stacking both doubles the cliché.Followers will not remember the brand as distinct. The face does all the work, and the system adds nothing.

Separate product brands. Family Law AI Assistant and Alexia stay distinct and disclosed.

02 / The idea was already in the documents

"Confidence is not the same as accuracy."

From the channel trailer script in the Social Media Strategy, section 13.

This line is the brand. Everything in the research report points the same way. The real enemy is a fluent answer that nobody has checked. The skill Andrew teaches is verification: keep the original, mark what is uncertain, and know whether a source actually supports the point. The identity should make that act of checking visible in every asset.

The method, as written in the foundation report

FACTObservable, with a source location.
ALLEGATIONSomeone's claim, attributed to them.
INFERENCEA reasoned reading, labelled as one.
UNKNOWNMissing information stays missing.

Plus two audiences: people with a lawyer and people without one, and a third path for limited help. Each route below takes this method and expresses it differently.

03 / Three routes

One idea, three different brands.

The routes sit at three points on a real spectrum: authority, access and provocation. They match the three audience territories in the original documents, but each one now has an idea, an ownable asset and a system.

Route 1 / Authority[E] EvidenceEvery claim gets a bracket. A citation becomes the logo. Route 2 / AccessNext StepA wayfinding system for people who arrive with a problem, not a legal category. Route 3 / ProvocationResolveFluent AI text is blurred. Checked text comes into focus.

Route 1 / Authority

[E] Evidence

Idea: every claim gets a bracket. Lawyers use square brackets to pinpoint a paragraph in a judgment, as in [95], and editors use them to mark an insertion. Here the E in Andrew E becomes a citation mark: [E] for Evidence. It works as a quiet double meaning. The name still stands for Easterbrook.

Legal AI with Andrew [E]

Why it is right

Why it is right

  • It turns the two learning routes, the core product decision, into the visual system.

Risks and demands

  • Close to a government-services look. Keep the founder's portrait prominent.

Why it is right

  • The most distinctive route in a feed and the most award-ready, because the motion idea carries the brand.
  • Strongest for LinkedIn commentary, short video and the professional audience (group D).

Risks and demands

  • It leans technical. It may feel cold to distressed consumers.
  • Electric blue keeps a faint tech-blue echo. The blur, not the colour, must do the work.

05 / Recommendation

Lead with Route 1, [E] Evidence.

It is the only route where the logo is the method. It is the most ownable, the cheapest to run alone and the strongest distinctive element for trade mark purposes. Pick one route and commit to it. The original documents warn against combining unrelated identities, then recommend a blend of A, B and C anyway. A blend is how the work became generic.

What would change this. If testing shows that consumers in groups A and C find the brackets intimidating, move to Route 2. If the channel mix shifts to short video and professional commentary, Route 3 becomes the stronger choice.

On the name: "Legal AI" is descriptive. Under the Trade Marks Act 1995 (Cth), a mark must be capable of distinguishing the owner's services, and descriptive words often face objection. The distinctive weight therefore sits on "Andrew E" and the device. Get a professional search and advice through IP Australia before you commit. This note is a general observation, not a clearance opinion.

Next steps

  1. Retire the three conflicting palettes. Record one decision in writing.
  2. Run a five-second test. Show each route on a real phone to five people from each audience group (A to D). Record recall, perceived trust and what each person thinks the brand does.
  3. Shoot the portrait. Follow the existing portrait playbook, and test it in a 110 px circle beside the compact mark.
  4. Draw the final wordmark. Kern and refine the chosen lockup. The bracket glyphs should be custom-drawn, not taken straight from a font.
  5. Build eight templates. Instagram post, carousel, Reel cover, YouTube thumbnail, banner, LinkedIn post, Starter Pack and newsletter header.
  6. Publish the behaviour. A dated corrections log and a source register go live with the brand.
