Polished. Premium. Not yet yours.

The four generated kits are consistent across platforms, cleanly laid out and much better finished than the PDFs. But they render the same generic navy-and-cobalt system the review rejected, and they carry three integrity problems that a brand about verification cannot publish. The CGI prompt would take you further in the wrong direction. Below are the findings, how award-level studios work differently, and repaired prompts.

05 / Your CGI prompt, red-teamed

Adjectives in, generator defaults out.

The prompt describes a rendering style for cars, jewellery and hardware. It has no subject, no layout, no text space and no exclusions, so the model fills the gaps with its defaults: chrome, glow and blue gradients. Each phrase, and what it actually does:

High-contrast cinematic CGI

"Cinematic" pulls towards film grading (teal shadows, orange highlights). "CGI" signals a render, which fights the real-photo portrait rule.

Replace
premium reflective materials, realistic specular highlights, sophisticated gloss finishes

It produces chrome, glass and lacquer: the AI-hype look. Your Identity Report warns against looking like "an AI consultancy rather than an approachable educator".

Redirect
colour-adaptive metallic reflections ... preserving its original hues

This contradicts itself. Metal reflects its surroundings, so hues shift. Generators also cannot hold exact hex values without neutral surfaces.

Remove
controlled luminous accents

It produces glowing lines and light streaks, the sci-fi AI tropes on your avoid list.

Remove
architectural precision and exceptional visual sharpness

Vague quality words have little effect. Precision comes from lens, light and composition instructions.

Make concrete
seamlessly adapted to any colour palette ... brand identity

A prompt that fits any brand is, by definition, not your brand. It needs your subject, palette roles and exclusions.

Make specific

What to keep from your instinct

Your wish for sharpness, high contrast and crafted materials is right. Point it at an object that means something, your own [E] bracket, rather than at abstract waves. Prompt P0 does exactly that.

06 / Engineered prompts

Ten slots, then plates, then type.

Generate parts without words. Set every word in Figma or Canva afterwards. Each prompt fills the ten slots below. They are written for the Proof palette (ink #121417, paper #FFFFFF, correction red #D4231B). To use Highlight instead, swap in lime #D4F27A.

1 PURPOSEWhat it is for, ratio, text space
2 SUBJECTOne concrete thing
3 COMPOSITIONThirds, empty zones, crop safety
4 CAMERALens, angle, depth of field
5 LIGHTSource, direction, hardness
6 MATERIALSPhysical and specific
7 PALETTEHex values with roles
8 STYLETechnique, not artist names
9 EXCLUDEClichés, text, people, blue
10 CHECKWhat must be true to pass
P0 / Your CGI prompt, repaired

The [E] bracket as a crafted object

Studio product photograph for a brand hero image, 16:9. Subject: a single sculptural object, a pair of square brackets enclosing a capital letter E, about 20 cm tall, cast in high-gloss red enamel (#D4231B) with thin brushed-steel edges. It stands on a sheet of heavyweight uncoated white paper (#FFFFFF). Composition: object on the left third, slightly turned 15 degrees; the right two-thirds stay completely empty for a headline. Camera: 100 mm macro lens look, eye level, f/8, everything on the object pin sharp. Light: large soft overhead source plus one hard rim light from the left that draws a crisp specular line along the enamel edges; one clean contact shadow in near-black ink tone (#121417). Colour discipline: the enamel stays pure red; all reflections are neutral grey and white only, with no blue, teal or warm cast. Style: editorial still-life photography, precise and restrained. Exclude: any other text or letters, logos, people, glow, light streaks, particles, bokeh orbs, waves, gradients, chrome background, blue tones. Check: the red reads as #D4231B, the background is clean white, the right two-thirds are empty.
Keeps your gloss, contrast and sharpness, but applies them to an ownable brand object instead of a generic abstraction. Use it for the website hero, channel trailer end card and slide covers.
P1 / Banner plate

Paper and one correction stroke

Background plate for a YouTube channel banner, 16:9, 2560 x 1440. Subject: a seamless sheet of white paper (#FFFFFF) with fine natural grain. In the lower-left corner, one hand-drawn red felt-tip stroke (#D4231B) striking through a short line, with a slightly textured ink edge. Composition: everything else empty; keep the central band (about 1546 x 423) completely clear. Light: even, soft daylight, no vignette. Style: flat, print-like, photographic paper texture, not CGI. Exclude: text, letters, faces, waves, gradients, glow, blue. Check: pure white ground, one red mark only.
Then add the wordmark and descriptor as live text in Figma. Recheck YouTube's current banner safe-area guidance before export.
P2 / Thumbnail plate

The marked page

Photograph for a video thumbnail background, 16:9. Subject: a printed A4 page of dense body text lying on a white desk, seen from above at a slight angle, with a red fine-liner pen resting across it. One paragraph is bracketed in the margin with red ink square brackets. Composition: page on the left 55 per cent; right 45 per cent is plain white desk for a headline. Camera: 50 mm, shallow depth of field; the bracketed paragraph sharp, the rest softly out of focus. Light: neutral window daylight, true whites. Palette: white, ink black (#121417), red (#D4231B) only. Exclude: legible words, case names, citations or numbers (all page text must be unreadable blur), people, laptops, gavels, scales of justice, blue. Check: no readable text anywhere.
Unreadable text is essential. A generator will invent case names and citations, which a verification brand must never publish.
P3 / Overlay marks

The editor's toolkit

Asset sheet of six isolated hand-drawn editorial marks on a pure white background, arranged in a 3 x 2 grid with generous spacing: a pair of square brackets, a single strike-through line, a loose circle for ringing a word, a margin tick, a double underline, a short curved arrow. Red felt-tip ink (#D4231B), confident single strokes, slight natural texture at stroke ends. Flat even light, no shadows, no paper texture. Exclude: text, letters, numbers, colours other than red. Check: each mark is separate and easy to cut out.
Remove the background, vectorise and save as reusable stickers in Canva or Figma. These marks become your recognisable on-screen language.
P4 / Real photo edit

Identity-locked background swap

Edit the attached genuine photograph of Andrew. Preserve the person exactly: face, skin texture, hair, expression, age, body shape and clothing must not change in any way. Do not retouch, smooth, beautify, sharpen features or alter proportions. Change only the background: replace it with a seamless light-grey paper backdrop (#EEEEEC) with a soft, natural shadow behind the shoulders, matching the existing light direction and colour temperature. Output a 4:5 crop and a 1:1 crop with the face in the upper third. Check: overlay the result on the original; the face must match pixel-for-pixel in structure.
Use only with a real photograph. Compare the result with the original side by side, and discard any output where the face has changed.
P5 / Red-team QA

Make a vision model check the output

You are a strict brand red-team reviewer for "Legal AI with Andrew E", an Australian education brand whose promise is verification. Assess the attached image against this brief. Palette: ink #121417, paper #FFFFFF, correction red #D4231B; no blue. Avoid list: scales of justice, gavels, robot or chrome heads, circuit lines, glow, silk waves, gradients. For each test give a score of 0 to 2, the evidence and a fix: (1) identity truth: is any person synthetic or altered? (2) integrity: any invented metrics, logos, endorsements, legible case names or citations? (3) palette fidelity: list the five dominant colours as hex and compare them to the palette; (4) forbidden elements; (5) text artefacts; (6) empty space where the brief requires it; (7) would the image still fit a competitor's brand? (8) is "premium" coming from craft or from effects? Return a table, a total out of 16 and PASS only if the total is 13 or more with no zero on tests 1 or 2. Do not soften findings.
Run it on every generated plate before you use it. Then show the final asset to five real people from your audience groups.
Universal exclusions

Paste at the end of any prompt

Exclude: text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, people, faces, hands, scales of justice, gavels, courtroom columns, robots, chrome heads, circuit boards, glowing lines, light streaks, lens flares, bokeh orbs, particles, silk or fabric waves, abstract swooshes, colour gradients, purple, blue, teal, neon.
Some tools take exclusions as a separate negative prompt field or a parameter (for example Midjourney's --no). Where none exists, keep this sentence at the end of the prompt.
07 / Skills to build

Where the quality gap actually is.

The gap between these kits and award-level work is not rendering power. It is idea, art direction, typography and critique. Generators make all of these easier to skip.

For you

  1. Art direction. Write a one-page shot list for a real portrait session: three crops, one workspace scene, one "marking a page" scene, all on the brand palette.
  2. Typography basics. Hierarchy, line length and headline kerning. Ellen Lupton's Thinking with Type is the standard primer.
  3. Template building. Figma or Canva components with locked fonts, colours and safe-area guides, so you never set a thumbnail from scratch.
  4. Plate-first prompting. Use the ten-slot anatomy and never generate words or people.
  5. A critic's eye. Run the swap, squint, cover, 168 px and contrast tests on every asset. Free contrast checkers such as WebAIM's do the maths.
  6. Hook writing. Turn list titles into tension: a claim, a doubt, a correction. Test two thumbnails where the platform allows it.
  7. Brand strategy fluency. Alina Wheeler's Designing Brand Identity covers the process top studios follow.

For Claude in this repository

  • wolff-olins-brand-craft (existing): diagnosis, organising idea, category escape, system design and pressure tests.

Order of work

  1. Decide the route and palette (recommended: [E] Evidence with Proof).
  2. Shoot real portraits.
  3. Generate plates P0 to P3, and run P5 on each.
  4. Build eight templates with live text.
  5. Test with five people per audience group.
Bottom line

Stop asking the generator to be the designer.

Award-level work comes from one owned idea executed with restraint. Use AI for plates and edits. Use a real photograph for your face. Set every word yourself. Then red-team the result as hard as you would an AI legal answer, because your audience will.
