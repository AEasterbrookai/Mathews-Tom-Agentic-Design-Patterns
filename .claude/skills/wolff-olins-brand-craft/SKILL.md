# Wolff Olins brand craft

A working method for producing brand identity at the level of the leading global consultancies. It distils publicly reported Wolff Olins practice and the craft standards that award-winning identity work consistently shows.

## 0. Evidence boundary (read first)

Keep three kinds of statement apart in every output.

| Type | What it covers in this skill |
|---|---|
| Verified (secondary sources) | Wolff Olins was founded in Camden Town, London, in 1965 by Michael Wolff and Wally Olins. Its stated mindset combines ambition for the work, optimism for the world and a fearlessness to "flip the script" (reported by The Drum, 2024). Reported work includes Tate (2000), London 2012 (launched 2007, publicly controversial), AOL (2009) and Uber (2018: simplified wordmark plus a custom typeface, Uber Move, drawn with MCKL, per Dezeen). Its current offer frames brand, culture and experience as levers of business transformation. |
| Inference (craft synthesis) | Sections 1 to 8 below. They describe how work of this calibre is built. They are not quoted Wolff Olins process documents. Never present them as the agency's proprietary method. |
| Unknown | Internal tools, fees and exact process steps. Do not invent them. |

Never claim to be, or to speak for, Wolff Olins in anything published. "In the manner of" is acceptable inside a working session; client-facing material should say "approach informed by".

## 1. Diagnose before designing

Read every source document on its own, then across the set. Produce a short diagnosis with evidence for each point.

1. **Truth inventory.** List what is specific and true about the subject: its method, audience tension, founder story, vocabulary, artefacts, rituals. The best ideas usually hide in the operational documents, not the brand deck.
2. **Category codes.** List what every competitor does (colours, symbols, type, tone). In legal: navy, serif, scales, columns, gavels. In AI: blue-violet gradients, glowing nodes, robot heads, circuit lines. Overlap of two categories doubles the cliché risk.
3. **Generic tests on the existing work.**
   - *Swap test:* replace the name with a competitor's. If it still fits, it is generic.
   - *Cover test:* hide the logo. Is the material still recognisable?
   - *Idea test:* can you state the brand idea in one sentence that implies behaviour? Lists of adjectives (credible, approachable, practical) are not ideas.
   - *Consistency test:* do the documents agree on colour, type and name usage?
   - *Accessibility test:* compute WCAG 2.2 contrast for every text colour. 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and UI marks.
   - *Rationale test:* does each design choice trace to strategy, or to taste presented as strategy?
4. **Keep what works.** Name the strong decisions worth carrying forward. A redesign that discards good research is vandalism, not ambition.

## 2. Find the organising idea

One sentence, rooted in a verified truth, that tells the brand how to behave and the designer what to make.

- Start with the tension the audience lives with. Then find the role the brand plays in resolving it.
- The idea must generate artefacts. If it cannot suggest a device, a voice line and a behaviour, keep going.
- Prefer ideas that are visible in the work itself (a method, a gesture, a mark people already use) over abstract values.
- Write it as a short line the founder could say on camera.

## 3. Escape the category deliberately

Break at least one dominant category code on purpose, and keep at least one so the audience still knows where they are. Record which codes you broke and why. Boldness belongs in one place; keep everything around it quiet.

## 4. Build a system, not a logo

A modern identity is a toolkit that works when the logo is absent.

| Layer | What to define |
|---|---|
| Idea | The organising sentence and the proof behaviours. |
| Signature asset | One ownable device (a gesture, a frame, a motion, a glyph) nobody else in the category uses. |
| Wordmark and marks | Primary lockup, compact mark, avatar treatment. Test at 32 px and in a 110 px circle crop. |
| Colour | 4 to 6 tokens with jobs, contrast ratios and proportions that follow use, not arbitrary percentages. |
| Type | A display face with character, a highly legible text face, optionally a utility face. Licence status noted. |
| Imagery | Photography rules, especially for founder-led brands: real, consistent, never synthetic presented as real. |
| Motion | How the signature asset moves. One orchestrated behaviour beats many effects. |
| Voice | Three to five principles with a do/don't pair each, plus signature lines. |
| Behaviour | What the organisation does that proves the idea (for example a public corrections log). |

## 5. Design for the real medium

Mock up at true sizes on the channels that matter: profile circle, mobile feed, thumbnail at 320 px wide, 2560 x 1440 banner (YouTube Help states a 1235 x 338 text and logo safe area at the 2048 x 1152 minimum, roughly 1546 x 423 when scaled to 2560 wide; recheck before production), document cover, slide. A system that only looks good on an A4 board is not finished.

## 6. Present three genuinely different routes

Three routes should sit at different points on a real strategic spectrum (for example: authority, accessibility, provocation). Each route gets:

1. A name and a one-line idea.
2. Why it is true for this client, citing the source material.
3. The signature asset and the system.
4. Applications at real size.
5. What it risks and what it demands (production effort, founder behaviour, cost).
6. A score against the client's stated brand qualities, with reasoning, not decoration.

Then make a recommendation and say what would change it. Do not hide behind "it depends".

## 7. Pressure-test before release

- Swap, cover and idea tests from section 1, now applied to your own routes.
- Squint and 32 px tests.
- Contrast computed, not guessed.
- Trade mark sense check: descriptive words (for example "Legal AI") are weak marks; the distinctive element must carry protection. Recommend a professional search, do not give the clearance opinion yourself.
- Claims check: no unverified credentials, outcomes or endorsements in brand copy.
- Production check: can the client make a week of content with this system in the tools they already use?

## 8. Writing standard

Plain English, short sentences, active voice. State facts, inferences and open questions separately. No hype words. Avoid long dashes as punctuation in client copy if the client prefers it.

## Output checklist

- [ ] Diagnosis with evidence and "keep" list
- [ ] Organising idea per route
- [ ] Category codes broken and kept
- [ ] Signature asset, wordmark, palette with ratios, type, imagery, motion, voice, behaviour
- [ ] Real-size applications
- [ ] Risks, demands and scoring
- [ ] Recommendation and next steps
- [ ] Evidence boundary stated
